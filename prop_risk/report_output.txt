  Two honest readings of this table:
  1. rho barely moves any number, and that is the lesson. Correlation
     does NOT change your expected yearly cash (E[sum] = sum of E[.]);
     it changes the shape of the path. Copying one idea into three
     accounts is ONE position sized 3x, so the size gets decided once,
     on the idea -- not three times, once per account.
  2. Sequencing, one-at-a-time vs parallel, is worth about 5%. Where you
     put your risk is worth 60x (A2 20 USD/yr vs A3 1294 USD/yr). Spend
     your decision-making on sizing, not on juggling terminals.

6. DOES CROSS-ACCOUNT HEDGING CREATE MONEY?
==============================================================================
  Opposite legs, same size, in two accounts. The P&L's are mirror
  images, so the losing leg dying is the winning leg's profit.
pair                                sigma net (closed at limit) net (firm eats the gap)
A3 (180) short A3 (180)                50                  -2.0                    -2.0
A3 (180) short A3 (180)               180                  -2.0                    27.6
A3 (180) short A3 (180)               600                  -2.0                   316.3
A3 (180) short A2 (15)                 50                  -2.0                    11.4
A3 (180) short A2 (15)                180                  -2.0                    77.7
A3 (180) short A2 (15)                600                  -2.0                   389.0
A2 (15) short A2 (15)                  50                  -2.0                    24.5
A2 (15) short A2 (15)                 180                  -2.0                   126.5
A2 (15) short A2 (15)                 600                  -2.0                   459.8

  Column 1 is what you actually get: zero, minus two spreads. Column 2
  only turns positive if a move jumps THROUGH the losing account's
  drawdown limit and the firm eats the overshoot. That is not an edge,
  it is a short-gap bet with two extra spreads -- and it is the exact
  pattern ('simultaneous opposite positions across accounts') that most
  firms void payouts for. Read your T&C before you even think about it.

  The transfer is real but it is not free: you convert two live, paying
  accounts into one account worth the same, minus costs, minus an
  account's remaining optionality. Hedging moves risk, it never deletes
  it, and it is only 'smart' when the sacrificed account is already dead
  anyway -- which is the same as saying: cut size on the one you were
  protecting, and keep the other.
